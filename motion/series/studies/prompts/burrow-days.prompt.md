Burrow Days · study 53 · video · 1152 frames at 24 fps, 120 bpm · palette "warren"
Style: A comic-book-rendered short film at a rabbit's-eye scale: flat cel-shaded forms whose shadows are HALFTONE DOTS, ink hatching on top, depth of field drawn as COLOUR MISREGISTRATION instead of blur, characters animated ON TWOS while the camera moves on ones, sound words (onomatopoeia) lettered into the scene as objects, IMPACT FRAMES, a sticker FREEZE, comic-PANEL splits, speed and focus lines, all inside a 2.39:1 cinemascope letterbox with a low, macro camera that makes grass blades trees and a walker's boots buildings. This is not a flat cartoon and not a gouache or ink-wash look: every form is a hard-edged flat fill with exactly one shadow shape, and the texture lives in the dots and the ink, never in a gradient. THE FRAME (pure function of F): landscape paints the picture in a 1920 × 804 window with deep bars of 138 px above and below; sound words may break over the bars (comic lettering breaking the frame line) but stay inside layout().safe; captions sit inside the picture. A faint print speck tile (seeded, computed once per size: about 6,000 specks of 1–2 px in ink and surface at 3–6% alpha) sits over the whole picture. TWO CLOCKS: characters read the rig time Tc = 2 · floor(F / 2) (on twos); the camera, speed lines, dust, sparkles and ambient drift read F itself (on ones). Every shot, cut and sound cue sits on the 12-frame beat; impact frames (2–4 frames), the sticker freeze, whip pans and sound-word pop-ins are drawn INSIDE a shot as functions of F, never as their own shots, so validate() only ever sees on-grid shots. DEPTH: each shot is a list of flat layers at depths z (sky ∞, far hedge 30, mid meadow 8, the rabbit's plane 4, near grass 1.5); a camera per shot, cam(F) = {x, y, zoom, tilt}, eased between keys; each layer draws offset by the camera times 1/z and scaled about the frame centre, so near grass sweeps past and the hedge crawls. MISREGISTRATION AS FOCUS: a layer's offset d = min(9, 7 · |1/z − 1/zf|) px at 1080 from the shot's focal depth zf (the rabbit's plane unless a beat says otherwise); an out-of-focus layer is drawn into an offscreen canvas once per frame, then composited as a cyan (accent2) tinted copy at −d, a magenta (accent) tinted copy at +d (each 'source-in' tinted, drawn at 55% alpha) and the true colours on top, so its edges fringe in colour instead of blurring; the in-focus plane is crisp. Fast movers get the same fringe along their motion vector, scaled by speed. This is depth, not damage: no slicing, no scanlines, no glitch. CEL SHADING AND HALFTONE: one fixed key light per scene (dawn low from the right, day high, dusk low from the left). Each form is its base fill plus ONE shadow shape (the form's outline offset 8–20% away from the light, clipped to the form) filled with the base darkened 18%, then a halftone of ink dots over that shadow: three dot tiles computed once per size (cell 8 px at 1080, screen angle 15°, dot radius cell · 0.5 · √v for v = 0.25, 0.45, 0.7); scenery dots are screen-locked; character dots are anchored to the rig's root with pattern.setTransform so they ride with the body and never crawl. On the lit edge a 3 px rim stroke (accent at dawn and dusk, surface by day) covers the side toward the light. Ink: an outer contour of 3–5 px on the shadow side only (a tapered centreline, w = w0 · sin(πs)^0.6), plus 3–6 short parallel hatch strokes at 45° along the shadow edge; no outline closes on the lit side. THE RABBIT (invented; the one full rig): side view, a tree of parts each drawn with ctx.save/translate/rotate from its parent: body (an egg, s7 agouti brown with a surface belly shape), haunch (a big circle at the rear), head (circle + muzzle), two long ears (tapered leaves with a pink-grey inner shape, pivots at the base), a large comic eye (surface disc, ink pupil, one surface glint, the pupil radius a closed-form value that shrinks in fear), nose, three whiskers each side, two forepaws (two segments), hind leg (thigh + long foot) and a cotton tail (a surface puff with an ink contour on its shadow side). All joint angles are closed-form functions of Tc: HOP (phase p = (Tc − t0) / 12 per hop, one hop per beat): airborne for p < 0.6 with body height −H · sin(πp / 0.6), body stretched 1.15 × along its axis and pitched −15° at take-off, hind feet trailing; contact for p ≥ 0.6 with a squash to 0.88 × and the feet planted; x advances by an eased step. GRAZE: neck pitched 35° down, jaw chew = 0.5 + 0.5 · sin(2π · Tc / 6), nose twitch = 3 px · sin(2π · Tc / 4), an ear flick (a 6-frame 20° flick and return) on the beats a beat names. ALERT: body pitched 60° up, ears straight up, twitch twice as fast. FREEZE: every angle held except the pupil tremble (±1 px on twos) and the ears laid flat back. THUMP: the hind foot lifts 30° over 4 frames and slams (a spring back with 15% overshoot), two ground rings expand from the foot (ink, 3 px, fading), the camera shakes 6 · e^(−t/5) · sin(2πt / 4) px. BOLT: bound cycle of 8 frames, body stretch 1.3 ×, ears flat back, the tail flagged up and bright surface. BREATHE: the ribcage scales 1 + 0.05 · sin(2π · Tc / P) with P easing from 8 to 24 frames. ONE QUADRUPED RIG serves both the fox and the dog: body, head, snout, two ears, a two-segment tail and four two-segment legs, with a parameter set per animal (the fox: long, low, s6 orange with a surface chest and tail tip, pointed ears, amber s1 eyes; the dog: taller, rounder, surface-and-ink patches, floppy ears, tongue out) and three cycles: stalk (one leg at a time, body 20% lower, tail straight), gallop (8 frames) and trot (12 frames). THE HAWK is a silhouette, not a rig: one closed path (head, hooked beak, two wings with five primary-feather fingers, a fanned tail) whose wing angle is a closed-form flap, halftone on its underside and a surface rim against the bright sky; ITS SHADOW on the grass is the same path scaled 0.35 in y, skewed, filled ink at 55% with a halftone edge. THE WALKER is only a pair of giant boots and trouser hems stepping through the frame (never a face, never a full figure) and a lead that swings from above. SPEED LINES: during runs, 30–60 seeded horizontal streaks (1–3 px, 60–400 px long, surface, accent and accent2 at 60–90%) moving 80 px per frame against the motion. FOCUS LINES: at a pounce, stoop or reaction, 90 seeded radial wedges in ink converging on a point, each 2–10 px wide at the frame edge, leaving a clear ellipse around the subject, re-seeded every 2 frames so they flicker on twos. WHIP PAN: 6 frames of horizontal streaks over a 60 px smear of the outgoing and incoming framings. IMPACT FRAMES (2–4 frames each): the whole picture flips to a flat graphic, a sunburst of 24 alternating s1 and surface rays, or a diagonal split of s1 and accent with the characters as pure ink silhouettes, with the sound word huge across it, then snaps back. THE STICKER FREEZE: the action stops; the rabbit is shown cut out with a 10 px surface sticker border and a hard 8 px ink offset shadow, inside an accent square tilted −6° over deep with drifting surface and s1 specks, a caption box beside it; it stays alive (the square breathes 1.00 → 1.02, the specks drift, the pupil trembles). COMIC PANELS: a page of cream (ground) paper with ink-bordered panels (4 px borders, 16 px gutters) that slide in one per beat; each panel is a shot function drawn in its own clipped box, never a still. SOUND WORDS: invented words only, Inter 800 uppercase, each letter rotated by a seeded ±8° and scaled 0.9–1.2 (the first letter largest), sheared −0.2 for lean, filled s1 or surface, a 6 px round-join ink stroke and an ink extrusion (the word drawn 8 times offset 1 px down-right in ink underneath); letters pop in 2 frames apart on a spring (scale 0 → 1.25 → 1.0), hold, then shake out; big words (a cry, a thump, a strike, a whistle) 120–220 px at 1080, small ones (a twig, a sniff, a clip) 72 px.
