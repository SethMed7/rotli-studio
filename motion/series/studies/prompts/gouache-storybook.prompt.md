--- THE STUDY (from series/studies/briefs/gouache-storybook.json) ---
Gouache Storybook · study 52 · video · 720 frames at 24 fps, 120 bpm · palette "gouache"
Style: An opaque-gouache picture book, animated: every shape is a flat, opaque patch of paint that carries its own brush direction, a few black dry-brush marks give it drawing, and the scenes change by being PAINTED ON (broad brush ribbons), by a push THROUGH a round window that holds another world, or by hard cuts on the beat. This is gouache, not watercolour or ink wash: no washes, no bleeding, no gradients, no transparency in the paint; value comes from a second flat shape, never from a blend. THE CADENCE (the reference's measured tell): the film is 24 fps but every fourth picture is held, so there are 18 new paintings a second. Every animated quantity reads the painted time T(F) = i − (i mod 4 == 1 ? 1 : 0) with i = floor(F), never F itself; cuts, ribbons' start frames and sound cues stay on F (cuts and cues sit on the 12-frame beat and ribbons start on multiples of 4, so a cut or a new ribbon is never a held frame). still-frames.mjs will count about a quarter of frames 'identical': that is the design, and no half-second window is ever still. No motion blur (paint is crisp); the one exception is the push through the glass (below). PAPER: the ground colour with a paper-tooth tile computed ONCE per size into an offscreen canvas (seeded: about 12,000 specks of 1–2 px in line and surface at 4–8% alpha, plus a low-frequency mottle), drawn over the finished frame at 6% so every scene sits on the same sheet. THE BRISTLE TILE, also computed once per size (512 × 512, seeded): about 900 thin strokes all running along +x, 1–3 px wide, 60–400 px long, slightly curved, alternately surface and ink at 3–9% alpha, with ragged ends: a colour-neutral streak map. A PAINTED SHAPE is: its outline sampled every ~12 px with each vertex pushed in or out by seeded noise of 0–3 px at 1080 (never vector-perfect); a flat fill; then, clipped to the shape, the bristle tile drawn rotated to the shape's own brush angle (a fixed per-shape angle that follows the form: horizontal across a sky, down a coat, round a hat) plus two or three 'load' streaks of the fill mixed 25% with surface where the brush was fullest; and on the trailing side 6–20 drag hairs, thin streaks of the fill 10–40 px long leaving the edge along the brush angle (the dry-brush fringe). Textures are locked to their shapes: nothing boils, a held painting is a still painting. SHADING is two flat tones: each form gets one shadow shape (its fill darkened 18% and pulled 20% toward s5) on the side away from a fixed upper-left light, and a highlight dab of surface at 50–70% with streaks (cheek glint, the glass's arc). Blush is an s7 dab of five overlapping streaks at 35%. INK is accent, not outline: black dry-brush marks only on the shadow side of a form and on defining lines (the dot eyes, the hat's ribbing, window frames, roof edges, the globe's rim), and no contour closes: each covers 60–85% of its perimeter. An ink mark is a centreline with width w(s) = w0 · sin(πs)^0.5, rendered as 4–6 parallel sub-lines across that width whose visibility along s is gated by seeded 1D value noise, so the mark breaks into bristle streaks where the brush ran dry, mostly near its ends. THE PORTAL is a snow globe: a glass circle on a small wooden base (s3 trapezoid with a dark grain streak); its world is drawn in the circle's own unit space and clipped to it; the glass is a thin surface rim at 50%, one broad curved dry-brush highlight arc in surface at 70% upper left and a short reflection streak lower right, with the rim's ink mark on the shadow side. EVERY WORLD is one function world(k, box, T) with two passes, paint (the painted shapes) and ink (the dry-brush marks), so the same world can fill the frame behind the kid, sit small inside the globe as a preview, or be drawn ink-only as its underdrawing: that single source is what makes each match cut through the glass exact. Worlds are few-shape landscapes (sea with a striped lighthouse; mustard dunes and a white sun; layered viridian jungle fans; indigo night pines on snow with star dabs; an ultramarine reef with vermilion fish and bubble rings; night rooftops with mustard windows; a cratered grey moon on deep with a ringed planet; patchwork fields under striped balloons) and the kid's HOME: a slate street of narrow houses in drizzle. BRUSH RIBBONS (the wipe that paints the next scene on): a ribbon is a cubic Bézier centreline that runs past both frame edges, a width of 220–360 px at 1080, a start frame and a 20-frame sweep; head h(u) = easeInOut(u) along the curve, tail = max(0, h − 0.55); the ribbon polygon is the centreline offset ±W/2 between tail and head, its edges ragged by seeded 1D noise of 0–10 px, its head split into 4–6 dry bristle tongues, painted in the ribbon's colour with the bristle tile oriented along the tangent. The next world is drawn clipped to the union of every ribbon swept so far and the old world outside it; after the last ribbon clears, the new world is full frame. The unpaint runs the same machinery, revealing the ink-only pass on bare paper. SNOW: about 140 flakes inside the globe (about 220 full frame inside the globe world); flake i has seeds x0, y0, r (2–6 px), v, ω, φ: y = (y0 + v · T) mod h, x = x0 + A · sin(ω T + φ); each shake at frame t_k adds, for T > t_k, a swirl offset S · e^(−(T − t_k)/18) along a per-flake direction and a vortex rotation about the globe centre of 1.6 · (1 − e^(−(T − t_k)/8)) · e^(−(T − t_k)/40) rad: closed-form sums over the shakes already past, never a simulation. Flakes are surface-coloured dabs (small rotated ellipses with one streak), not glowing dots. RAIN: dry-brush dashes (1.5 px, 30–60 px, surface at 35%) on a seeded grid at 15° falling 26 px per painted frame, wrapped mod the frame height. THE KID (invented): a small kid in a mustard (s1) knitted hat with a vermilion pom-pom, an ultramarine (accent2) duffel coat with three surface toggles, a vermilion scarf, s6 skin, two ink dot eyes each with one surface glint, s7 blush dabs, a small ink mouth arc, mustard mittens. Built from separate painted parts, each with its own pivot (head, hat, pom-pom, scarf, torso, two two-segment arms, mittens, legs), drawn with ctx.save/translate/rotate from its parent; poses are keyed joint angles eased between keys; a 6 px head bob on every beat. Blink: the eyes' height × 0.1 for 4 frames. Eyes widen: scale 1 → 1.5 on a spring (about 12% overshoot). A shake jolt at t_k moves the globe by 26 · sin(2π(T − t_k)/6) · e^(−(T − t_k)/10) px vertically, the arms follow it, the pom-pom lags on a damped spring. The scarf streams in wind as a ribbon (same renderer as the wipes) whose centreline is a travelling sine, y = a(x) · sin(kx − ωT) with a growing along its length: hair-into-paint-stroke grammar, in a scarf. THE CAMERA is one transform over the world. The push through the glass and the pull back out are exponential about the globe's centre, scale = 14^ease(u), the globe centre travelling to the frame centre; once the globe's radius passes the frame's half-diagonal, draw the inside world full frame (the match cut). During the fastest 8 frames of each, add a radial zoom smear: three extra copies of the frame at ×0.96, ×0.92, ×0.88 about the fixed point at 25%, 15% and 8% alpha. Holds carry a slow 1.00 → 1.03 push and a 2 px sine drift, so no painting is ever frozen. THE IRIS: the outside of a circle is filled with deep, its radius closing from the frame's half-diagonal to the globe's radius + 30 px; its edge is painted, not geometric: the radius wobbles by seeded angular noise of ±6 px and about 40 dry-brush streaks of deep sweep tangentially across it. TYPE: one line in the whole film, the end title 'Shake for Elsewhere.' in Instrument Serif 400 at 120 px (landscape at 1080), surface on deep, fading up over 12 frames, no plate. Nothing else is written; the pictures carry the story.
Learns from: A painted animation shared on X by @Artedeingenio (https://x.com/Artedeingenio/status/2104618081029152958), 30 s at 24 fps: opaque gouache-look illustration with bristle-streaked flat fills and black dry-brush accents; pictures held one frame in four (18 new images a second); round portals that hold other worlds and match cuts THROUGH them; a centred, locked figure whose world hard-swaps behind it on the beat; broad paint ribbons that sweep across and repaint the scene; a moment where the colour drains to its ink drawing; and a brush-edged iris to black at the end.
