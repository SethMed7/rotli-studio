Literal Type · study 51 · video · 1920 frames at 60 fps, 120 bpm · palette "mono"
Style: Seven words, each of which DOES what it says, played as one continuous take with no cuts: every prop becomes the next scene's first shape. PAPER AND INK ONLY: the palette's ground is the paper and its ink the ink for the first three quarters; at the flip (frame 1440) they swap, so ink becomes the ground and ground the type (the palette has no dark key: ink IS the night). accent and accent2 are never used, not even for a highlight: emphasis is only weight (outline letter vs solid), value (muted vs ink), size and position. muted is for ghost and outline letters, dashed cluster outlines, axis labels, leader-line annotations and the HUD, never for a line that matters; wireframe fills are ink at 6–14% alpha over the ground (after the flip, ground at 8–16% over ink). THE DRAFTING GRID: a square construction grid under everything, 48 px minor lines (ink at 5% alpha, 1 px) and a major line every fourth (ink at 10%), running off every edge; after the flip, ground at 6% and 12% over ink. THE CAMERA: one camera transform over the whole world, grid included: a slow roll between −2.5° and +2.5° and a scale drift between 1.00 and 1.05 on long sine periods (about 7 s and 11 s), so the grid is visibly tilted in most frames and holds never freeze; plus two big moves: an accelerating zoom-through (scale k^t, about ×14 in 30 frames) and the ball falling at the lens. THE HUD sits OUTSIDE the camera, top right inside the safe area, JetBrains Mono 500 at 24 px in muted: a live readout 'ss.cc s   frame nnnn   120 bpm' computed from F every frame (texture, not a line that matters). TYPE: two families only. Each WORD is Instrument Serif 400 at 170–190 px at 1080 (never under 150), default tracking, ink; outline letters are the same glyphs stroked 2 px with no fill. Everything technical is JetBrains Mono 500: the TYPED CAPTION under each word at 44 px (it types left to right at 2 frames per character, and a solid block cursor, a drawn rectangle about 0.6 em × 1 em, blinks 15 frames on and 15 off and stays after the line completes until the section leaves); counters, labels, leader-line annotations, axis labels and ruler readouts at 28–32 px (texture). EVERY WORD ENTERS ITS OWN WAY, and the entrance is the meaning (placed along a tangled path, dropped in like data, typed as outlines, spaced by a ruler, darkened by votes, filled by a ball, carried round a ring, dropped by a moving dot); never the same fade twice. PROPS are drafting-style line drawings: ink hairlines 2–3 px with round joins, flat fills, rounded corners 10–28 px; a crosshair is a 2 px ring with four short ticks; leader lines are 1.5 px with a 5 px dot at the anchor; dashed outlines use a 6/6 dash. The only shadow is one very soft flat offset under the phone (ink at 6%, 10 px down, no blur filter). MOTION: letters and props land on springs with a small overshoot (about 6%), the camera eases in-out, the zoom-through and the fall-at-the-lens are exponential, the ball flies on true parabolas with squash (1.4 × 0.7 for 3 frames at contact) and stretch along its velocity near contact, and onion-skin ghosts of the ball (1.5 px muted rings every 6 frames over the last 36) trace its arcs. Every section lands its payoff on a named beat and then HOLDS while its caption types; the camera drift keeps the hold alive. GLYPH LAW: the pack's JetBrains Mono has → ↑ × · but no U+2212 minus and no ✓, and Instrument Serif has no arrows: write time zones with an ASCII hyphen ('UTC-5'), draw every tick mark, the block cursor, the resize handle arrows and the placeholder X as strokes and rects. Any frame is computed from F alone: path progress, letter placements and rotations come from seeded hashes, never Math.random.
Learns from: A motion-design film by @yanliudesign shared on X (https://x.com/yanliudesign/status/2104758344305246506), which its maker says was made entirely with Claude: seven words that each act out their meaning with one line-drawn prop, zero cuts (each prop becomes the next scene's first shape: a zoom through a square, a crosshair that turns into a phone, a button that collapses into a ball, a ball that falls into the lens and turns the film dark), a display serif for the word and a monospace for everything technical, typed captions with a block cursor, a tilted drafting grid under a rolling camera, a live seconds/frame/bpm readout, and pure black and white with one inversion. Its seven words, its subtitles, its 'product design' subject, its wireframe, browser and matrix content, its greys, its fonts and its sound are not used; the words, props and copy here are Oriel's and invented.
