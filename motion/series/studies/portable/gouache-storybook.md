THE LOOK
An opaque-gouache picture book, animated. Every shape is a flat, opaque patch of paint on warm paper: a slightly irregular edge, a fine streak texture running in the shape's own brush direction (across a sky, down a coat, round a hat), a few lighter streaks where the brush was full, and short dry hairs trailing off one edge. Value comes from one flat shadow shape on the side away from an upper-left light and one pale highlight dab, never from a gradient. Black is used sparingly, as dry-brush marks that taper at both ends and break into bristle streaks where the brush ran dry, only on shadow sides and defining lines, and no outline ever fully closes. Pictures change 18 times a second (every fourth frame repeats the last), which gives hand-painted motion its slight stutter. The story is original: a small kid in a mustard knitted hat with a red pom-pom, a blue duffel coat and a red scarf stands on a grey, drizzly street holding a snow globe with a lighthouse inside. Shaking it paints the world over: three broad brush ribbons sweep across and wherever they pass the street becomes the sea and the lighthouse, and the scarf streams in the wind. The globe then shows the next world before it happens: in a montage the kid stands still with the globe held high while the world behind cuts on every beat through dunes, jungle, night pines, a reef, rooftops, the moon, balloon fields and all of them at once. The camera pushes through the glass into a tiny snowy square, where the kid's giant face peers in through the curved glass and taps it; the colour lifts off to the bare ink drawing and is painted back as evening; the camera pulls back out to the kid's own street, now dry, with snow on the hat; inside the globe is that same street with a tiny kid holding a tiny globe. A brush-edged iris closes onto the globe on black and the one line of text appears: 'Shake for Elsewhere.'

MOTION RULES
- Ease everything: ease-out for arrivals, ease-in for exits, a spring with a little overshoot for anything that lands. Ambient motion (a breath, a drift, a spin) may be a smooth repeating wave; nothing else moves linearly.
- Hook in the first second. No dead air: every hold keeps moving (a slow push-in, a drift, a breath).
- Nothing overlaps unless it is meant to. Keep text inside the safe margins and never smaller than 22 px at 1080 px on the short side.
- Paint reads as paint when every flat shape carries its own brush direction and a dry edge; and when the transitions are themselves painting (a stroke that paints the next scene on, a round window that already holds it), the cuts stop being edits and become part of the picture.
- Sound (Web Audio, starts on the first click): a driving 120 bpm loop whose drums enter at the montage, a hit on the first jolt, the lighthouse landing, the end of the montage, the tap on the glass, the repaint and the closing iris; whooshes into each brush sweep, each push through the glass and the iris; soft ticks for jolts, blinks and every montage cut; a three-note sign-off.
