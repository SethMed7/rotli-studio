THE LOOK
A comic-book-rendered short film at a rabbit's-eye scale in a 2.39:1 cinemascope letterbox. Every form is a hard-edged flat fill with one shadow shape on the side away from the light, and that shadow is filled with halftone ink dots; ink contours and a few hatch strokes sit only on the shadow side, and a thin rim of light (magenta at dawn and dusk) traces the lit edge. Depth of field is drawn as colour misregistration, not blur: anything away from the focal plane is drawn three times, a cyan copy offset one way, a magenta copy the other and the true colours on top, so near grass blades and the far hedge fringe in colour while the rabbit is crisp. The characters move on twos (their poses change every second frame) while the camera moves every frame. Sound words are lettered into the scene in heavy, leaning, extruded capitals that pop in letter by letter; 2–4-frame impact frames flip the whole picture to a sunburst or a diagonal two-colour split with the characters as ink silhouettes; a sticker freeze cuts the rabbit out on a tilted magenta square; comic panels split the frame for a stalk and a chase; speed lines and radial focus lines carry every run and pounce; short yellow caption boxes name each chapter. The camera sits at grass height, so blades are trees and a walker is only a pair of giant boots. The story is original: a wild rabbit wakes in its burrow at dawn, hops out and grazes; a hawk's shadow crosses, it freezes, the hawk stoops, it thumps and bolts into a bramble; a fox stalks it and pounces on empty ground, loses it in a zig-zag and bumps its nose on a fence; on a footpath a walker's off-lead dog sniffs it out and gives happy chase until it tumbles and a whistle calls it back; at dusk the rabbit hops home past the warren's waiting ears and dives into its hole. The picture shrinks into the last panel of a comic page of the day, and a yellow band reads 'BURROW DAYS', with 'one more day, and home.' beneath it.

MOTION RULES
- Ease everything: ease-out for arrivals, ease-in for exits, a spring with a little overshoot for anything that lands. Ambient motion (a breath, a drift, a spin) may be a smooth repeating wave; nothing else moves linearly.
- Hook in the first second. No dead air: every hold keeps moving (a slow push-in, a drift, a breath).
- Nothing overlaps unless it is meant to. Keep text inside the safe margins and never smaller than 22 px at 1080 px on the short side.
- A comic look is a set of substitutions, not a filter: shadows become dots, blur becomes misregistered colour, sound becomes lettering, emphasis becomes an impact frame, and time runs at two rates (characters on twos, the camera on ones), so a code-drawn scene reads as a printed page that moves.
