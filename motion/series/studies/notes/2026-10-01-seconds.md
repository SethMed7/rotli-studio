# Seconds: eight styles in four seconds each

> A new section of the studies: four-second loops, one visual style each, pushed to a premium finish. Chrome, frosted glass, a flow field, one-bit dithering, op art, Truchet tiles, paper folding and light trails, each researched, briefed from one template, built by one agent and scored by another. What a loop this short demands, and what each style needs before it reads as itself.

*Field note 03 · 2026-10-01 · rotli studio. Everything here can be re-run from this repository.*

## Why four seconds

The first 55 studies run 8 to 120 seconds. They teach structure: a hook, a turn, a payoff, a story told in shots.
A loop of four seconds teaches the opposite. There is no story to hide behind, no second scene to fix the first;
the style itself has to hold the eye, and the last frame has to become the first without a seam. It is also the
shape most short-form feeds reward: a piece met mid-motion, watched three times before the viewer notices it
repeats.

So the section keeps one template and changes only the style:

- **4 s, 60 fps, 120 bpm**: 240 frames, a 30-frame beat, two bars. The strongest moment lands on the downbeat of
  bar 2 (frame 120); something lands on frame 0, because a loop is met mid-motion.
- **A seamless loop**, picture and sound: `tools/loop-seam.mjs` must print SEAMLESS, and `beatScore` runs with
  `loop: true` so its tails wrap.
- **No words.** The style is the piece.
- **Square first, a vertical designed for the tall frame**, never a crop.
- **Closed form.** Anything that wanders (particles, strands, noise) is sampled from periodic functions of
  t = frame / 240, typically noise sampled on a circle in time (cos 2πt, sin 2πt), so frame 240 equals frame 0 and
  any frame renders on its own.

## The eight styles

**56 · Chrome.** Liquid chrome, the Y2K way. Chrome reads as chrome only through what it reflects, so the piece
fakes reflection mapping in 2D: a closed ribbon built from cross-sections whose surface normals pick colours from
an environment strip (floor, a hot horizon line, sky, sunset), plus a specular crest and a rim light. Sources:
[Y2K aesthetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Y2K_aesthetic), [reflection mapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflection_mapping).

**57 · Frost.** Glassmorphism done properly: the glass is defined by what it does to what is behind it. Each pane
shows the colour field behind it blurred (drawn small offscreen and scaled back up, no `ctx.filter`) and nudged
toward its edge, with an edge highlight, an inner glow and grain. Source: [glassmorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glassmorphism).

**58 · Flow Field.** Strands traced through an angle field of layered noise, like a plotter drawing in ink. The
field loops because time enters the noise as a circle, and every strand is traced fresh each frame from a seeded
start, so the piece is organic and exact at once. Source: Tyler Hobbs,
[Flow fields](https://www.tylerxhobbs.com/words/flow-fields).

**59 · One Bit.** Ordered dithering as a whole visual language: a lit 3D object rendered small, then thresholded
against an 8×8 Bayer matrix into two colours. The pattern stays locked to the screen, not the object, so motion
shimmers. Sources: [ordered dithering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordered_dithering),
[Return of the Obra Dinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Return_of_the_Obra_Dinn).

**60 · Op Art.** A regular grating displaced by one smooth warp field, crossed by a second grating at a slightly
different angle: the page breathes and moiré slides, and the eye supplies the motion. Sources:
[op art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Op_art), [moiré pattern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moir%C3%A9_pattern).

**61 · Truchet.** Smith's quarter-circle tiles join into continuous paths; rotate each tile by a delayed spring and
whole paths appear to re-route in a ripple, though only tiles ever turn. The loop closes because every tile turns a
whole number of quarter turns. Source: [Truchet tiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truchet_tiles).

**62 · Fold.** Folding is rotation about a line. A paper sheet as flat flaps, each turning about its crease with a
spring, shaded by its normal against one light, with creases that stay after the fold: a square becomes a plane,
glides and unfolds. Source: [origami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origami).

**63 · Light Trails.** A long exposure without history: each light's trail is its path sampled backwards over the
last moment, drawn fresh every frame, with light added where trails cross and a glow from a small offscreen pass.
Lissajous and harmonograph curves with integer frequency ratios close in 240 frames. Source:
[light painting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_painting).

## Method

Each style has a brief in `series/studies/briefs/` (family `seconds`) written from one shared template, so the
builders differ only in the style. Flow Field was built first as a pilot to test the template end to end; the
other seven followed from it. Every study was built by one agent from its generated prompt and scored by a second
against `workflows/critique.md` until every criterion reached 8; the rounds are in `critiques/`.

## What the second reader found

No first build shipped: all eight came back "fix". Every style read as itself at once (readability 7 to 9,
accuracy 7 to 10 in round one), so the template carries a style well. What it did not carry was the shape of a
four-second loop. The same four misses came back in nearly every first round:

- **Frame 0 did not land.** Builders made the loop seamless and stopped there; a seamless loop that starts in
  drift reads as a clip joined mid-way. Frame 0 needs its own small event, in picture and in sound (rule 7).
- **The hit was too light.** The strongest moment was drawn as the brightest frame, not as the end of a gesture:
  no wind-up, a flare that washed out instead of snapping, a cue barely above the bed. A hit needs an approach
  (a pre-pulse on beat 3, a gather, a held breath) and about 6 dB of headroom in the sound (rules 9 and 10).
- **The second half went still.** After the hit, the piece eased to rest and waited for the wrap. Two seconds is
  half the loop; it has to keep evolving (rule 8), and a hold has to keep a little life inside it.
- **The vertical was a crop.** The tall frame was the square's motif re-centred, with open bands above and below.
  Composition scored lower in the vertical than the square in six of eight first rounds; each needed the motif
  re-thought for the tall frame (a climb instead of a glide, a path that runs top to bottom, a sun placed high).

Variety was the lowest score in round one (6 or 7 for every piece), and it is the criterion this format is about:
in four seconds, variety is not new scenes but a gesture that keeps changing across three watches in a row.

## Lessons for the engine

- **A pilot pays for itself.** Flow Field took four rounds and produced rules 1 to 6; the seven builds that followed
  it took two or three, and none repeated a pilot mistake (no blur on fine lines, no envelope that missed the wrap,
  no 2D noise asked to move in time). Rules 7 to 10 came from the first critic rounds of the next pieces and were
  written back into every brief before the builders' next pass.
- **Measure the sound unweighted.** LUFS says the mix is right; it does not say the hit stands out. A 10 ms RMS
  envelope against the bed's median shows the hit, the frame-0 cue and the pre-pulse as numbers a critic can hold
  a builder to.
- **The critic can be wrong, and the builder should say so.** In Flow Field the critic's fix for a direction glitch
  (interpolating angles) would itself have torn the field at the seam; the builder refused with the reason, and
  the critic agreed. In Fold the builder narrowed the flight's arc and turned the vertical into a climb, because a
  dart seen nose-on reads as a heart. A reasoned refusal is part of the loop, not a failure of it.
- **When the fixes stop converging, look at the model, not the moves.** Fold took six rounds. Four of them tuned
  the camera, the timing and the light around a plane that only read as a plane in profile; the critic finally
  named the cause: one nose fold leaves rectangular wings. Re-folding it as a real dart (corners to the centre
  line twice, then in half, then the wings), with the crease pattern computed by clipping and reflecting the
  sheet, fixed readability from every angle in one round. Paper folding is the one style here where the subject's
  geometry, not its rendering, carries the read.
- **The lossless seam check matters more than the summary.** `loop-seam.mjs` compares the wrap step to the largest
  inner step, so a piece with a hard hit passes easily; comparing the 239 → 0 step with its neighbours on lossless
  frames is the real proof.

## Seconds in 3D: the first character

Study 64, Pop, takes Seconds into three.js: Pip, an invented creature built only from three's primitives, lit like a
feature-animation short, pops a soap bubble with the sprout on its head. Before any brief, a spike answered the one
question that could have stopped it: can WebGL keep "every frame is a pure function" and exact-hash goldens? It can.
The same frames hashed identically on four pages in each of two browsers with no extra flags, so the 212 existing
goldens never moved. `kit/three.ts` (a scene built once, every animated property set from the frame number) and
`tools/determinism.mjs` (that 8-page proof, for every 3D piece) came out of it.

It took nine critic rounds, the most of any study, and the shape of the misses is the lesson. The mechanics landed
early (squash and stretch that keeps its volume, one gravity for every hop, the eyes leading the head, the sprout's
overlap), and the gates passed from round one. What took eight more rounds was acting and staging, a frame or two at
a time:

- **The camera can cancel the action.** A camera that rises with a jump erases the jump. The fix that worked was a
  camera that follows the leap a few frames late, and then staging rather than optics: with the bubble hovering
  above Pip, the feed's safe bands capped Pip's size, until the bubble moved beside Pip at crown height, nearly
  grazed the sprout and escaped upward, which also gave the crouch a reason.
- **Effects read as demos before they read as physics.** A white glow disc, opaque pearl droplets, a camera-facing
  ring, a clipped dome, a hoop that left a halo, emoji-like star glints: each round's pop was a recognisable effect.
  What finally read was the physics of a film: intact until it is touched, a torn edge that runs round it, a soft
  mist, and a few clear beads with a glint.
- **Faces fail on the in-betweens.** Shut eyes drawn as skin caps read as swollen domes, half-open frames read
  sleepy, and a lower lid with a straight edge read as bags. The fixes were flush lash arcs for shut eyes, snapping
  through the in-between frames, and keeping the lower lid out of a surprise entirely.
- **Symmetry is the last tell.** Arms that mirror each other read as a rig. A lag fixes motion but not holds; a
  held pose needs its own asymmetry. And anticipation goes opposite to the action: arms pressed down before they
  swing up.
